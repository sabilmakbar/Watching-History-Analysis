Problem and decision context

The source repository held a large anonymized playback export but no analysis explaining where viewing demand concentrates, which contexts are associated with completion, or whether those patterns generalize to later events. This report turns the export into an auditable portfolio artifact and highlights where product or data-quality investigation would be most useful.

Data and approach

The pipeline parsed 106,823 logical records, accepted 106,811 structurally valid events, and recorded 12 rejections without changing the source. Exploratory analysis covers time, platform, content, audience context, completion, and robust playback-quality percentiles. A leakage-controlled benchmark then compares a prior dummy with Logistic Regression on 67,874 labeled VOD and catch-up events using an 80/20 chronological holdout.

The export covers only 1–16 February 2020 in UTC, with partial first and last dates. Treat the results as evidence for investigation within this sample.

Data trust

Every parsed logical record is accounted for. The build accepts rows with exactly 41 fields, rejects 12 malformed records with their logical and physical source positions, and verifies the source SHA-256 before analysis.

All times are UTC. The original repository does not record the upstream publisher or license, so this repository is the available source and provenance remains limited.

Viewing time

Question: When does playback demand concentrate by UTC date and hour? play_time supports event-volume timing, but it cannot recover each viewer's local time.

Daily volume

Hourly volume

Evidence: Daily volume reaches 8,473 events on 2020-02-12. The 13:00 and 14:00 UTC hours are the busiest pair at 8,166 and 8,195 events, while the first and last dates cover only partial windows.

Next question: Is the midday UTC peak stable across complete weeks and viewer time zones? Operational monitoring can start with this window, but the 16-day export is too short for seasonality or boundary-day comparisons.

Platform and content mix

Question: Where does playback start, and which content types create that volume? Platform and content fields support event mix; they do not measure unique-user reach.

Platform mix

Content mix

Evidence: Web mobile supplies 57.8% of events and reaches 82.1% when combined with the Android app. VOD contributes 63.2%, while livestreaming contributes 36.5%; catch-up is the small remainder.

Next question: Do mobile surfaces dominate because of audience preference, content availability, or measurement coverage? Prioritize their reliability for this sample, while keeping event share separate from viewer share.

Completion

Question: Among events with a usable outcome, where does completion differ by platform and content category? The source supports this only for labeled VOD and catch-up events.

Denominator: only VOD and catch-up events with a defined completed label. Every hover label reports or corresponds to its eligible event count.

Evidence: Android-app completion is 32.1% across 7,840 eligible events versus 23.7% across 53,680 on mobile web. News reaches 35.0% on 21,283 events, compared with 19.7% on 21,846 for Entertainment.

Next question: Does the platform gap remain after matching content and audience context? These observational associations identify where to investigate, not which platform or category causes completion.

Audience context

Question: Do login, premium, and ad flags coincide with different completion rates? They describe event context, not stable user histories or treatment assignment.

Evidence: Logged-in events complete at 31.2% versus 24.0% when logged out. Ad-present and ad-absent rates are nearly identical at 24.8% and 24.9%; the higher premium rate rests on only 501 eligible events.

Next question: Which content and platform combinations explain the login gap? Use premium cautiously because of its small denominator, and do not read any flag difference as a causal effect.

Playback quality

Question: Do the playback fields contain viewing-experience signal, and are the fields themselves usable? Robust outlier treatment shows the 1st through 99th percentiles because raw tails otherwise hide both questions.

Evidence: Average bitrate is fixed at 300,000 from the median through p99, while buffer duration rises from 1 at the median to 1,035 at p95 and 3,590.8 at p99. Another 4,946 bitrate values are missing.

Next question: Does 300,000 represent a cap, default, or source unit, and what unit governs buffer duration? Resolve the data dictionary and capping behavior before treating these distributions as operational experience thresholds.

Model evidence

Question: Can information available when playback starts predict later completion better than the class prior, without using outcome-derived fields?

The benchmark trains on 54,299 earlier eligible events and tests on 13,575 later events. The holdout begins 2020-02-13T15:48:31+00:00; equal timestamps use retained logical record number for deterministic ordering.

Evidence: Logistic Regression improves PR-AUC from 0.218 to 0.365, Brier score from 0.172 to 0.158, and reaches ROC-AUC 0.690. Its strongest coefficients are signed associations conditional on the encoded inputs and use model log-odds, not causal effects.

Next question: Do these gains persist on a longer, newer holdout with stable field definitions? The current model is a credible benchmark for discrimination and probability quality, not an operational decision system.

What we found

  1. Most playback is mobile. Web mobile is 57.8% of events and reaches 82.1% with the Android app.
  2. The clearest demand window is 13:00–14:00 UTC. Treat the first and last dates as partial coverage.
  3. Completion varies materially by context. Android app versus mobile web is 32.1% versus 23.7%; logged in versus out is 31.2% versus 24.0%.
  4. Content mix matters. News is 35.0% on 21,283 eligible events versus Entertainment at 19.7% on 21,846.
  5. The benchmark adds signal. PR-AUC moves from 0.218 to 0.365; Brier score moves from 0.172 to 0.158.
  6. Quality fields need definition checks. Bitrate plateaus at 300,000 and buffer duration reaches 1,035 at p95.

These are event-level associations from a short export, not causal or user-level effects.

Method

The build validates the source, parses timestamps in UTC, coerces numeric and boolean fields, and creates aggregate results without writing event-level cleaned data. The completion model uses only playback-start context, an 80/20 chronological split, most-frequent categorical imputation, one-hot encoding with rare levels grouped below 20 observations, and Logistic Regression with a prior-probability dummy baseline.

Caveats

The data covers a short 2020 window, identifiers are anonymized, and the repository does not establish the original publisher or license. Results describe associations in this export; they do not support causal claims, personalized recommendations, or broad population inference.
